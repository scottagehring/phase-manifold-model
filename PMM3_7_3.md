## Abstract

The Phase Manifold Model (PMM) describes physical reality through complete events organized as Before, During, and After. One complete shared event is a Field Transition Unit:

Fₜᵤ = B ∘ D ∘ A

B carries pre-spatial possibility, D carries active spatial realization, and A carries the completed persistent record. PMM treats space and motion as two aspects of the same emergent relational realization: a realized relation supplies spatial separation, and evolution of that relation supplies motion. PMM treats time as angular and depth as the relation between two D vantages, distinct from ordinary height and width. Its event architecture follows the four c-order grades c⁰ through c³. Contact @ opens D; anti-contact −@ closes it through the terminal vantage lock.

Each elementary pairwise interaction is one FTU. Its completed event produces two lineage-endpoint A records with one shared event identity and one event-level source energy. Active depth ends at transcription, while the completed x-y readout and phase history remain available as record. The three phase-time readouts retain their branch-dependent rank and conditional physical interpretations; their temporal metric remains open.

Across many FTUs, completed records accumulate in Φ and condition later interactions through the relational source and clock-response sector. Action provides a common measure across B, D, and A. Candidate assignments, recovery bridges, and open dynamics are distinguished from adopted principles throughout the manuscript.
