## Abstract

The Phase Manifold Model (PMM) proposes that physical reality develops through events. Possibility becomes an interaction, and a completed interaction leaves a record that contributes to later events. PMM calls these stages Before (B), During (D), and After (A). One complete event involving both participants is a Field Transition Unit (FTU):

Fₜᵤ = B ∘ D ∘ A

Before contains possibilities without active space. Contact, written @, begins During.

The interaction develops through two internal planar descriptions, called D vantages. Their relationship produces internal depth.

Anti-contact, written −@, ends During and transfers the completed event into an After record. PMM describes event progress with an angle and treats space and motion as aspects of realized relationships.

Its c-order ladder, c⁰ → c¹ → c² → c³, assigns grades to the passage through B, D, and A.

Each elementary pairwise interaction is one FTU. Completion produces a separate A record for each participant’s continuing history, or lineage.

The two records share one event identity and one event-level source energy. Active depth ends when the record is written; the completed x-y description and the history of the event’s phases remain.

The model assigns time values to B, D, and A. How many of those values can vary independently depends on the stated assumptions and whether the event is active or complete.

PMM follows this process within one FTU and across a network of FTUs. Completed records accumulate in the history field Φ, influence later contact, and condition the timing of subsequent interactions.

The Angular Temporal Recursion Principle postulates the connection c³ → Φ → c⁰ between distinct events. Contributions can interfere when their relative phase is preserved.

Action provides a common measure for the three stages.
