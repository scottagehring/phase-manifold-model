1. Purpose and Goals

PMM proposes an underlying event architecture connecting quantum mechanics (QM), general relativity (GR), and information theory (IT). It organizes potential, active realization, and completed record as phases of one event. These are PMM correspondences, not claims that the three established theories are identical.

The primary goal is this connecting architecture. The secondary goal is to investigate whether force can be manipulated through depth; this is a research objective, not an established capability. Resolving known physics problems is a further possible outcome of the first two goals.

2. One Complete Event

A Field Transition Unit (FTU) is one complete Before → During → After event:
Fₜᵤ = B ∘ D ∘ A
B = Before = Potential → Quantum Mechanics → Wave → Uncertainty → Pre-spatial
D = During = Realization → General Relativity → Particle → Partial Certainty → Spatial xyz
A = After = Transcription → Information Theory → Cube → Full Certainty → Post-spatial

Uncertainty, partial certainty, and full certainty describe the event-state heuristic: a completed outcome is definite as a record. They do not imply complete knowledge of every microscopic detail. The A cube is a record representation, not active three-dimensional A-space.

In PMM, @ actualizes an admissible pair interaction and opens D. At −@, D reaches terminal closure; C₃ governs transcription into persistent A records. One shared pair interaction is one FTU, with two separate endpoint Afters associated with its participant lineages. They share one event identity and one event-level source energy.

Here Φ is accumulated record history, δ is inter-participant distance, and Σ is closure spread. Internal depth zᴰ is distinct from δ.
