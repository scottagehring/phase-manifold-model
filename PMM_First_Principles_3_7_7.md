## 1. Begin with an event

The Phase Manifold Model (PMM) follows an event from possibility through interaction. When the interaction ends, its record persists.

PMM calls these stages **Before, During, and After**:

**Possibility → Interaction → Record**

The model proposes this event sequence as a common foundation for quantum mechanics, general relativity, and information theory. Its central ideas are that time is angular, space emerges through interaction, depth has a distinct origin, and completed events contribute to what can happen next.

The technical manuscript gives the full equations, assumptions, and derivations.

## 2. Before, During, and After

**Before (B)** carries the possibilities for an interaction. Its spatial relationships have potential form.

**During (D)** is the realized interaction. This is where PMM places active space, motion, and the development of the event.

**After (A)** is the completed record. The record retains information about the completed interaction.

One complete passage through these stages is a **Field Transition Unit**, or **FTU**:

Fₜᵤ = B ∘ D ∘ A

The symbol ∘ joins the three stages into one event. They are phases of one event.

PMM associates Before with the wave description of quantum mechanics, During with realized geometry and the particle description, and After with information and record. These are the model's proposed correspondences.

### The phase manifold

The **phase manifold** is the connected geometry through which PMM describes an event’s passage from possibility to realization to retained record. Before, During, and After are its three phases. One complete passage through them is a Field Transition Unit.

Its geometry extends across the whole event, while active three-dimensional space exists during the During phase. Before carries possible spatial relationships; During realizes them through its two internal vantages; After retains the completed result as information.

“Phase” names these different conditions of the event. “Manifold” names the connected geometry that holds their relationships together. Across multiple FTUs, completed records accumulate in Φ and condition subsequent events, connecting individual event passages into an ongoing physical history.

## 3. Contact starts the interaction

PMM uses **@** for contact: the point at which an allowed pair interaction becomes actual. It uses **−@** for anti-contact: the boundary where During ends and the event is recorded.

B → @ → D → −@ → A

The model groups the conditions around an event into three parts:

- C₁: what can enter the interaction.
- C₂: what governs the interaction while it develops.
- C₃: what is retained when it ends.

Each elementary interaction between a pair is one FTU involving both participants. At completion, each participant receives its own record of that event. The two endpoint records refer to one event identity and one event-level source energy.

A participant can continue through many events. PMM calls that continuing history a participant lineage. Several compatible pair interactions can occur concurrently, including interactions involving the same lineage; each is a distinct FTU.

## 4. Space exists during the event

In PMM, space is realized through interaction. Before contains possible spatial arrangements. During realizes three-dimensional geometry. After retains information about the completed arrangement.

| Stage | Spatial meaning |
|---|---|
| Before | Possible geometry |
| During | Active three-dimensional geometry |
| After | A retained record of completed geometry |

The technical shorthand is:

Fₜᵤˣʸ = (ψ(x,y,z), xyz, xy)

Here ψ(x,y,z) describes spatial possibility. The xyz entry describes active space. The final xy entry describes the retained planar information after active depth ends. Before uses potential coordinates; After uses retained planar information.

PMM therefore assigns active spatial dimensionality **0 → 3 → 0** across the event. The record describes completed geometry as information.

The **space window** describes how spatial manifestation opens after contact and closes toward anti-contact. It describes access to realized space, whose internal geometry has its own defining relations.

## 5. Depth, length, and distance

PMM distinguishes three spatial quantities that can otherwise be easy to confuse.

**Depth belongs inside one FTU.** During has two internal x-y descriptions, called **vantages**. Each has a scale determined by its height and width. Their relationship gives the event's depth:

Fₜᵤⱽ¹ = √(x₁ᴰy₁ᴰ),  Fₜᵤⱽ² = √(x₂ᴰy₂ᴰ)

zᴰ = L = √(Fₜᵤⱽ¹Fₜᵤⱽ²)

The symbols Fₜᵤⱽ¹ and Fₜᵤⱽ² name the two vantage scales. L is their common geometric scale, and zᴰ is internal depth. The vantages are internal parts of the event's geometry. Active depth ends when During becomes After.

Length is the traversal length used in one FTU's time calculation. Its symbol is Fₜᵤᴸ. Under the specified assumption that the During time equals its action divided by energy, this length equals L. That equality is conditional.

Distance separates participants in the network of events. Its symbol is Fₜᵤᴿᵢⱼ, where i and j identify the participants. The collection of these distances is called the δ network. These distances define participant separation in the δ network.

## 6. Space and motion develop together

PMM's **Motion Operations Principle**, abbreviated Mₒₚ, says that space is realized relation and motion is the change of that relation.

For a pair of participants, their separation describes a spatial relationship. How that separation changes describes relative motion. How the rate of change changes describes relative acceleration.

Before contains possible relations and motion. During realizes those relations, and After records the completed interaction.

This principle connects the meaning of space and motion. A specified dynamical law determines the evolution of an interaction.

## 7. Time is angular

PMM describes an event's progress using an angle. During begins at θᴰ = 0 and reaches its adopted completion at θᴰ = π.

The angle measures progress through the event. The angle describes the internal progression of the event.

PMM assigns a time value to each stage:

Fₜᵤᵀ = (Tᴮ, Tᴰ, Tᴬ)

These values describe different aspects of the same event. Their definitions are:

Tᴮ = [ℏ/(mₚc²)]Δθᴮ

Tᴰ = Fₜᵤᴸ/c

Tᴬ = Δθᴬ/ωᴬ

Δθᴮ and Δθᴬ are the Before and After angular spans. ωᴬ is the frequency retained in the After record. The constants ℏ, mₚ, and c are the reduced Planck constant, Planck mass, and speed of light.

The model's current span assignments use the original probability P of the selected contact:

Δθᴮ = arccos√P,  Δθᴬ = −ln P

Because both spans depend on the same probability, they are linked. PA-B and PA-A assign these spans to phase action.

The three time values can vary independently over the nondegenerate range when traversal length is an independent input. They also have three degrees of freedom during the active event under the conditional length-equals-L assumption.

At completed closure under that assumption, at most two can vary independently. These results count independently varying time values for the chosen inputs and event phase.

## 8. The Ladder of Light

PMM represents the passage through an event with four c-order grades:

c⁰ → c¹ → c² → c³

- c⁰: Before.
- c¹ and c²: the two internal During vantages.
- c³: After.

The Planck mass mₚ, speed of light c, and Boltzmann constant k_B mark the boundaries and roles that PMM calls **phase creases**. In the model, mₚ marks entry, c sets the realization scale, and k_B converts information into entropy units. Their accepted physical values are inputs to PMM.

**“Light is stillness”** expresses PMM's use of c as the invariant reference within During. Light follows a null path, along which proper time is zero. PMM describes event progress with its During angle.

## 9. Action measures the three stages

In physics, **action** has units of energy multiplied by time. PMM uses it as a common measure across Before, During, and After:

Sᴮ = ℏΔθᴮ,  Sᴰ = ℏθᴰ,  Sᴬ = ℏΔθᴬ

During's adopted completion at θᴰ = π therefore gives:

Sᴰ_cl = πℏ

The two During vantages describe the same During stage. They use one During action value.

PMM uses the three action values in two different ways.

**The Action Ledger adds them:**

Fₜᵤˡᵉᵈ = Sᴮ + Sᴰ + Sᴬ

The result has ordinary action units, J·s.

The proposed Action Cube multiplies them:

Fₜᵤˢ = Sᴮ × Sᴰ × Sᴬ

The result has units (J·s)³ and describes the product of the phase-action coordinates. The After record cube has its own definition.

The product is also separate from counting events. With the current span assignments, a probability-one contact makes the product zero at completed event closure.

## 10. Energy continues into the record

PMM assigns mass and energy descriptions to the three stages. The adopted During-to-After frequency relation gives:

Eᴬ = ℏωᴬ = Eᴰ

This expresses energy continuity within the event under that relation. The two endpoint records refer to the same event energy.

The mass and energy formulas describe the event once its energy is specified. The interaction energy enters as a physical input.

## 11. Completed events become history

After records accumulate in **Φ**, PMM's symbol for accumulated history.

A completed record retains quantities associated with the event, including frequency, completed planar shape, and an information value based on the contact probability. These support reconstruction of completed scale and shape. The reconstruction uses the explicitly retained record quantities.

The **Angular Temporal Recursion Principle** proposes that accumulated history contributes to the Before possibilities of later events:

B → D → A → Φ → next B

Each FTU completes its own Before–During–After sequence. Φ connects completed events to subsequent events.

Video playback illustrates how recorded information can help produce a later realization: the record supplies information, while the playback equipment supplies present energy. Interference requires the additional condition that contributing channels preserve relative phase. The preparation map specifies how Φ enters the receiving Before state.

## 12. From many events to gravity

The single-FTU description follows the inside of one event. The multi-FTU description follows the relationships among events and participant histories.

PMM describes gravitational response through differences in the rate of During angular progress. It compares a local rate with a reference rate, using the same comparison time:

κ = (dθᴰ/dt)/(dθᴰ/dt)₀

The ratio κ defines a **clock potential**, φ:

φ = −ln κ

A spatial change in that potential gives the gravity description:

Fₜᵤᴳ = ∇φ,  g = c²∇φ

Here ∇φ means how the clock potential changes across space. Multiplying by c² gives acceleration units.

These equations organize PMM's gravity framework. The calculation uses a source-extraction rule, event-network dynamics, and a specified geometry as physical inputs.
