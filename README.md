# Phase Manifold Model

> **Time is angular. Space is emergent relational motion. Depth is different. c is stillness.**

The **Phase Manifold Model (PMM)** describes physical reality through events: a possibility becomes an interaction, and the completed interaction leaves a record. PMM relates this event sequence to quantum mechanics, general relativity, and information theory.

The **phase manifold** is the connected geometry of an event's passage through Before, During, and After. “Phase” names these conditions of the event; “manifold” names the geometry connecting them. One complete passage is a Field Transition Unit (FTU).

Before contains the possibilities for an interaction, including potential spatial relationships.

During is the realized interaction. Its two internal planar vantages realize active three-dimensional space, with motion and angular progression within the event.

After retains the completed result as information. The event's active geometry ends, while its record persists.

Each elementary pair interaction is one FTU involving both participants. At completion, each participant receives its own record, with both records referring to one event identity and one event-level source energy.

Completed records accumulate in Φ and condition subsequent events, connecting individual event passages into an ongoing physical history:

Before → During → After → accumulated history → next Before

## Central principles

- Time is angular. PMM measures progress through an event with an angle. The model assigns separate time descriptions to Before, During, and After.
- Space emerges through interaction. Realized relationships supply spatial separation; changes in those relationships describe motion.
- Depth is different. An event's internal depth comes from the relationship between its two internal planar descriptions, called vantages. Depth is distinct from traversal length and distance between participants.
- c is stillness. PMM uses the speed of light as the invariant reference within During. Light follows a null path with zero proper time.
- History contributes to subsequent events. Completed records accumulate and influence later possibilities.

The manuscript follows these ideas at two scales: inside one event, and across a network of events and continuing participant histories. Its gravity framework connects differences in event timing with gravitational response.

## Event quantities

The manuscript assigns quantities to each phase of an FTU. The entries in each tuple follow the order Before, During, After.

| Quantity | Expression |
|---|---|
| Spatial description | Fₜᵤˣʸ = (ψ(x,y,z), xyz, xy) |
| Time | Fₜᵤᵀ = (Tᴮ, Tᴰ, Tᴬ) |
| Mass | Fₜᵤᴹ = (mₚ, Eᴰ/c², ℏωᴬ/c²) |
| Energy | Fₜᵤᴱ = (mₚc², Eᴰ, ℏωᴬ) |
| Phase-Action Vector | Fₜᵤˢᵛ = (Sᴮ, Sᴰ, Sᴬ) |
| Action Ledger | Fₜᵤˡᵉᵈ = Sᴮ + Sᴰ + Sᴬ, in J·s |
| Proposed Action Cube | Fₜᵤˢ = SᴮSᴰSᴬ, in (J·s)³ |

Here mₚ is the Planck mass, Eᴰ is the During energy, and ωᴬ is the frequency retained in the After record. The symbol ℏ is the reduced Planck constant, and c is the speed of light.

Action measures each phase: Sᴮ = ℏΔθᴮ, Sᴰ = ℏθᴰ, and Sᴬ = ℏΔθᴬ. The Action Cube multiplies these phase-action values.

Internal depth zᴰ comes from the two During vantages. FTU Length Fₜᵤᴸ is the traversal length used in the time calculation, while FTU Distance-Range Fₜᵤᴿᵢⱼ measures separation between participants. The manuscript defines each quantity and states the conditions under which they are related.

## Events, history, and gravity

The single-FTU description follows contact, internal geometry, and completion. The multi-FTU description follows relationships among events and participant histories, with completed records accumulating in Φ.

PMM describes gravitational response through a ratio κ of local During angular rate to a reference rate, measured against the same comparison time. This ratio defines the clock potential φ = −lnκ and the acceleration readout g = c²∇φ, where ∇φ measures spatial change in that potential.
